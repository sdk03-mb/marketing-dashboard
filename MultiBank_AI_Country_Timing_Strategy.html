What we cannot say yet

“This country is profitable”

Customer deposits are not MultiBank’s profit. We need income and cost figures to decide which country earns the most.

One important correction: the old Overview adds some averages and costs per account together. That can give the wrong country ranking. The country comments below use the separate Social and PPC figures. A recorded zero funding cost means “check the result,” not “we got a customer for free.”

How to split the timing

SplitSimple questionExample of the decision
Salary datesDo results change around verified salary dates?Test education and support around those days. Do not assume everyone is paid on the 25th or at month-end.
Weekly cycleWhich weekday and local hour work best?Compare Monday with other Mondays before moving spending to a “better day.”
Monthly cycleDo results improve at the start, middle or end of the month?Check the first, second, third and final parts of each month. Allow for different numbers of days and different ad budgets.
SeasonsDo weather, travel and school breaks change results?Test Australia’s and Europe’s holiday periods separately.
Time of yearDo named holidays or annual events change results?Test Ramadan/Eid, Christmas/Easter, Diwali or other relevant local events. Use confirmed dates.
TrendsAre customer interest, ad prices or sign-up results changing?A rise in searches can justify a small content test. It does not prove that more spending will make a profit.

Simple rule: first find a country and channel that work. Then learn which timing makes them work better.

The August file has no daily history, so it cannot tell us the best weekdays or salary windows. Request daily results for several months; use more than one year where possible to check annual patterns.

Why this approach makes sense

Research supports checking timing, allowing for delayed results and using controlled tests. It does not prove which MultiBank country will win.

What research tells usWhat we should do
Spending can change around income payments. Bills and other factors also affect it.Check salary patterns; do not assume they cause more trading deposits. Gelman and colleagues, 2014
Weekly patterns, seasons and holidays can affect business results.Learn each country’s calendar from its own daily history. Taylor & Letham, 2017
Advertising results can arrive later, and extra spending may produce less extra benefit.Wait for customers to finish sign-up and funding. Increase spending in small steps. Jin and colleagues, 2017
A fair comparison helps show whether advertising caused an improvement.Keep a similar group without the new campaign or spending change. Compare the extra results. Vaver & Koehler, 2011
When a controlled test is unavailable, models can estimate what might have happened without the change.Label that answer as an estimate and explain its assumptions. Brodersen and colleagues, 2015
